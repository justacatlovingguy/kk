WORKFLOW DESIGN

AI IT Director

A support assistant that drafts acknowledgments, organizes evidence, and helps technicians decide what to do next.

A fictional support request

“The shared printer stopped working after a workstation restart. I need to print a shipping label today.”

Director draft: “I’ll help narrow this down. Can anyone else print? Is the printer showing an error? Which workstation is affected?” The assistant asks for scope and urgency before proposing a priority.

From intake to closure

  1. Triage. Record who is affected, the business impact, and what changed. Flag any unknowns.
  2. Recommend. Draft an acknowledgment and propose a read-only check using an approved support procedure.
  3. Approve or escalate. The technician validates the recommendation and approves any changes. If several users are affected or the issue needs specialist access, route it to the designated service owner with the symptoms, impact, checks completed, and open questions.
  4. Verify. Ask the user to confirm a test print works from the affected workstation. Record the outcome in the ticket and name any follow-up owner.

Roles and boundaries

The AI proposes priorities, communications, and next actions. A technician owns the diagnosis, communications, access decisions, changes, and closure. This page illustrates a design using a fictional case.
