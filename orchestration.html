AI Orchestration Engine

See how Mercury routes work, how Yes / No / Maybe challenge a claim, and how Croizier assembles an evidence-backed result.

How it works

Croizier frames the task and owns the final recommendation. Mercury routes a bounded question to the Yes, No, and Maybe roles. Yes looks for supporting evidence, No looks for contradictions, and Maybe identifies gaps or limits in scope. Croizier compares the results and records the basis for its recommendation.

What a run would record

The design calls for versioned role instructions, the references each role used, its recommendation, and any abstention or failure. A second pass is reserved for a specific unresolved issue, with a proposed maximum of two passes. Agreement prompts a check of the evidence and a human reviewer can reopen the result.

Illustrative run

A release note says a feature was added in version 4. The compatibility guide limits it to one edition. Yes finds the release note, No challenges the claim that every edition is covered, and Maybe asks which edition the user runs. The output records that condition and the next check.
